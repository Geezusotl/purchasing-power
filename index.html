05 / SOURCES & METHOD

Good data.
Clear boundaries.

An independent project by Giovannie Sosa. Built to make public economic data easier to read and use.

Where the numbers come from

All observations come directly from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Data is monthly, not real-time. The dashboard checks for updates daily when its scheduled workflow is enabled. Preliminary values and historical revisions are retained.

    How the calculations work

    Annual change = (current value ÷ value in the same month a year earlier − 1) × 100. Real hourly pay growth = ((earnings now ÷ earnings then) ÷ (adjusted CPI now ÷ adjusted CPI then) − 1) × 100.

    The comparison chart rebases each seasonally adjusted series to 100 in its first common month. The calculator uses unadjusted CPI: equivalent amount = original amount × target CPI ÷ starting CPI. It compares exact months, rather than mixing annual averages with partial-year data.

    What this cannot tell you

    National averages do not describe every household, place, or job. Average hourly earnings are not median earnings and exclude non-private-sector workers. Changes in worker composition affect the average. CPI measures price changes, not differences in living costs between locations. These charts do not establish what caused a change.

    Missing months and update reliability

    No observations are invented or interpolated. An annual change is unavailable when its exact year-earlier month is missing. A failed or incomplete data request keeps the previous valid snapshot. Each series shows its own observation month; a recent update check does not imply newer data.
